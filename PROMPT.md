# HWY 99 Cannabis Co. — Homepage Design Prompt

> A three-paragraph creative brief used to direct the single-page homepage
> design. Focus is on feeling, atmosphere, and abstract reference points
> rather than technical specification.

---

Design a glassmorphism-driven single-page homepage for **HWY 99 Cannabis Co.**, a craft dispensary set along the rural Highway 99 corridor in Eugene, Oregon — and let the entire experience feel like the relief of pulling off a long stretch of road into a hushed clearing of old-growth Pacific Northwest forest. Translucent layered panels should float over an earthy, sun-warmed backdrop of real botanical imagery — tightly cropped cannabis leaves, resinous trichome-glittering flower, and the dappled green of Willamette Valley undergrowth — so each surface reads as honest glass refracting nature rather than a flat UI. As visitors arrive, the mood should be calm but quietly electric: the comfort of returning to a place that already knows you, layered with the slow anticipation of discovery. Scrolling through this single cohesive page should feel like a continuous walk through one space at golden hour — moss-deep greens, terracotta sun, brass-gold highlights, and pops of fresh chlorophyll lime woven in to keep the palette alive, never sleepy.

Typography should feel hand-stamped and assured — capitalized display type with the confident weight of a vintage highway shield, set against soft, humanist body type that reads like a knowledgeable friend at the counter rather than corporate copy. Interactions and animations should behave like real glass and real water: smooth and liquid, with parallax that breathes, hover states that bloom and refract, and scroll-triggered reveals that drift upward as if rising through clear resin — nothing snappy, nothing harsh, everything organic and gently inevitable. The single-page journey should progress emotionally from arrival to allegiance: a hero that feels like a warm welcome and a deep inhale; a Daily Deals strip that delivers a small, satisfying jolt of generosity; a featured-strains and categories passage that builds connoisseurship and trust; a story section that grounds the brand in soil, hands, and locality; and a Visit / Find Us close that ends in the simple, certain pull of *"yes, I'm coming in"* — one continuous narrative with no jarring seams.

Anchor the aesthetic in the abstract feeling of Pacific Northwest old-growth at first light, the quiet ritual of a Japanese tea house translated into a craftsman cabin, and the refined patience of small-batch artisanship — third-wave coffee roasters, natural-wine cellars, the unhurried elegance of an apothecary or natural-history reading room where every object earns its place on the shelf. Borrow the warmth of mid-century roadside Americana and the considered minimalism of contemporary Scandinavian wellness spaces, with the textural soul of raw linen, hand-thrown ceramics, oiled walnut, and brushed brass softened by morning fog. These references should steep the final single-page homepage in a sense of *grounded sophistication and unhurried craft* — premium without being precious, modern without being cold, deeply local without slipping into rustic kitsch — so that the visitor leaves feeling they have not just browsed a dispensary site but stepped briefly into a place they would actually want to drive to.
